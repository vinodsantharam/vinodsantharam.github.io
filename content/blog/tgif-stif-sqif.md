**TGIF:** Thank God It's Friday. The quote, the reply, the status of most of my Fridays over the last decade.

**STIF:** Sloppy, Toast, It's Friday. My mental state for the last six months.

## TGIF

I once worked at a company where important meetings (technical, decision making, analysis) were forbidden on Fridays, especially in the afternoon. I don't even want to talk about production releases. I think (and hope) nobody ever approved one.

At the same company, Friday was the day of the agile closing ceremonies: the stakeholder demo, the team retrospective and something I miss a lot, team building. It wasn't a TGIF of relief. It was a Friday I was grateful to live. Productivity, efficiency and alignment: those are the words that describe the "corporate" value of that Friday.

## STIF

There is a pattern I have noticed, and discussed with two engineer friends: Friday looks like the worst day of the week for productivity, efficiency and alignment.

The mental fatigue I talked about in a [previous article](/blog/state-of-coding-june-2026/) builds up all week long. On Friday, the debt comes due, and it's a huge one.

Say I'm working on a feature that needs a refactor, or that keeps growing the more I touch it. The coding agent (whatever or whoever it is) starts preparing a PRD, plans and other documents. By Friday, the energy I have left to read, understand, refine and judge is close to zero.

Friday, 3 p.m.\
The agent drops a long plan.\
I scroll.\
I read the same paragraph twice.\
I don't remember the first time.\
My eyes are open. My brain is not.

So what happens is:

> I trust you, do it.

It feels like grounding your kid and taking the iPad away, then handing it back just to get a moment of peace. You've had an exhausting week. You're not in the mood to negotiate, or to yell. You give up. In French we call it _l'usure_: you don't lose the fight, you just get worn down.

I'm drained on Fridays. I don't want to read the verbose output to make a decision. My inner voice tells me something isn't right. And then that decision stays stuck in my head the whole weekend.

Monday, guess what? I undo everything I did on Friday. This time my mind is clear, sharp, alert. I don't hand over the iPad! I adjust the decision tree until we reach the right implementation. And I feel satisfied, sometimes even proud, that I stepped back, whatever it cost in time and tokens.

The problem is the repetition. This pattern has happened too often. It hurts my productivity, efficiency and alignment (from a project management perspective).

## SQIF

**Side Quest It's Friday!**

I've decided to stop doing hard work on Fridays. There are always tasks waiting to be popped off the pile. Now on Fridays I work on:

- tooling
- documentation
- process
- bug fixes (not P0 or P1, of course)
- UX experiments and sketches
- things outside my scope, like working with DevOps to improve the pipelines, looking at the monitoring, thinking about new alerts and dashboards

I started yesterday. I felt great at the end of the day. Today is Saturday, November 7th, and there is no sloppy design stuck in my head to figure out.

Of course, not every Friday will be a side quest day. The idea is to manage yourself at the end of the working week, with whatever options you have, without compromising the team's goal.

Don't get me wrong: the sloppy outcomes on those Fridays are on me, not on the AI. I can't imagine doing software engineering the old-fashioned way anymore. With my background and experience, having AI as a super tool is something to be grateful for, as long as I use it on the main quest!
